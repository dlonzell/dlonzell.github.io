Danilo Symonette

I’m a PhD student in Computer Science at Stanford University, advised by James Landay. My research is at the intersection of human–computer interaction, artificial intelligence, and learning.

I earned an AS in Computer Science from the College of Southern Maryland in 2017 and a BS in Computer Science from the University of Maryland, Baltimore County in 2020. Before Stanford, I worked at the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, developing online curricula and personalized training tools and teaching Python, data science, and AI.

Outside of research, I enjoy boxing and playing piano.

Research interests

  • How can people take a more active role in shaping their own learning?
  • What intellectual work is valuable for learners to participate in when AI can do more for them?
  • How can generated simulations and multimodal interfaces connect exploration, practice, and reflection?

Research

GELLI: learner-authored language practice

GELLI generates narrative practice worlds from learners’ goals. Learners create a scenario, converse with a character, and use language support and reflection in mixed reality. Our study examined learning outcomes alongside agency, imagination, and discovery.

ProjectarXivPDF

Presence and learning in classroom VR

With Cyan DeVeaux and Jeremy Bailenson, I’m investigating classroom VR through interviews, observations, and a comparison of group and solo 360° video experiences.
